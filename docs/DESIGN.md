# Little Say interface

Little Say is a touch-first app for a family's boards. New devices start with language selection and optional starter-board generation. Existing libraries open to saved boards and their Play actions. Conversational creation lives behind New board, with a separate understated Build manually action beside it on the boards screen; editing lives with the board or tile it changes. These app conventions apply to native apps and the browser app at `/app`.

The public website homepage at `/` has a separate marketing layout in `src/website/LandingPage.web.tsx`, selected only by `src/Entry.web.tsx`. It shares the mountain identity and palette, describes implemented features, and links into the browser app. App Store and Google Play placeholders say **Coming soon** and have no destination until listings exist. Website copy and styles must never be imported by the native entry. New installed web apps start at `/app`; older standalone installs at `/` still bypass the homepage.

- Use the shared tokens in `apps/mobile/assets/brand/colors.json` through `src/ui/theme.ts`: deep evergreen text, a forest-green action color, a warm cream background, and six bright tile colors. White groups separate parent controls without heavy borders or shadows. The same palette colors the native launch screen and installed web app.
- Little Say's mountain is a friendly, rounded, wooded peak with a smaller foothill and a golden trail toward the top. Its smile makes the challenge welcoming. This is a playful reference to Little Si and Mount Si, not a literal trail map. Use the mascot with the Little Say wordmark in parent headers, and on the launch/loading screen and empty library. It is decorative, never a navigation button or another tile. Keep the child's board free of additional branding chrome. Do not add a tagline, achievement system, or progress metaphor to the usable interface.
- Use the system sans-serif font. Titles are bold and compact. Rounded tiles, generous targets, and familiar pictures provide the playful character.
- Name the action or content directly: New board, Edit tile, Saved voices, Play languages. No slogans, marketing subtitles, decorative eyebrows, or landing-page sections.
- Keep the latest played board in a larger card above the regular grid. Its preview and Play again button both start play; keep Edit and the board menu available. A filled heart marks a favorite. Pin favorites immediately below the spotlight, with no duplicate spotlight card, then show other boards; use recent play order within each group. Use the board's actual tiles and title without promotional copy. Favorites and play history are device metadata, independent of content edits, copies, and undo.
- Show explanation where it helps a decision: inline AI data-use disclosure, missing translations, storage failures. Keep recurring controls concise.
- Tapping outside a modal follows its close/cancel action; taps and gestures inside it do not dismiss it. Dismissing the leave-board confirmation keeps play active, and only an explicit confirmation leaves the board. AI workspace navigation releases pending requests and microphone capture; its page back action retains save guards.
- First-time setup has two functional steps: languages and starter boards. Save completed drafts for resuming, show the generated words, and offer a manual start when AI is unavailable. Never replace existing libraries with sample content or advance past a failed save.
- AI creation and editing use a full-page route with a conversation and independent board canvas. Keep the transcript scrollable and message/voice controls fixed above the keyboard. On narrow screens, stack the canvas above the conversation. Preserve the canvas during clarification; stream downloaded pictures into cards as they finish. Distinguish queued work from active animated cards and completed pictures, respect reduced motion, and offer stop/retry plus explicit final save. Put picture settings in the page header, accessible during work; changes apply to the next update.
- Keep the board editor on one tablet page: a compact responsive tile preview with AI, add, audio, and board actions beside it in landscape or below it in portrait. Tile position controls belong in the tile dialog. Small windows may scroll the preview without hiding the action controls.
- Use the platform photo picker and square cropper on iOS/Android. Browser uploads and later adjustments use direct drag/pinch interaction with fine zoom. Show the actual selected photo region in a large tile preview, and preserve that square region across thumbnails, play layouts, and device rotation. Crop cancellation must leave the previous image untouched; duplicate cards keep independent framing.
- Play prioritizes the tile grid. Its single-row header has the board title, a segmented language toggle when configured, and Hold to unlock. Keep the language toggle immediately left of the lock control. Playback state is shown on the tiles, not repeated in header text.
- Play and editor previews share measured, ordered card rectangles with balanced rows: row counts differ by at most one, with a soft preference for square-ish cards rather than fixed columns. Play fills its available area without scrolling; editor cells stay compact and may scroll in a small preview. Keep targets at least 44 logical units per side. If a play window cannot fit them, explain that more room is needed and recover when it resizes. Reflow cancels contacts tied to old geometry without restarting speech or changing card order.
- Speech uses a forest-green outer outline and speaker badge. A touch briefly shows an amber inset outline. Both can be visible at once without changing geometry.
- Preserve large touch targets, visible selection states, native accessibility labels, forgiving swipe activation, and the separate hold/release/confirm exit sequence across platforms.
- The parent hold's readiness label and release validation must use the same gesture state and monotonic clock. Cancel its timer and reset feedback together on movement, extra touches, native cancellation, backgrounding, or board interaction. Never announce release readiness from an independent timer.

Keep photos, user labels, and actual boards visually prominent. Add interface text only when it helps someone use the app.

## Pictures

Keep picture controls in parent flows: a workspace header settings button, a board Pictures panel, and expandable card Picture options. Show Picture source as one labeled selector with a current-value explanation. Expand choices vertically with descriptions for Automatic, Library only, AI illustrations, and Emoji only. Do not use wrapping option chips. Keep boolean controls separate as switches, and show the inherited source explicitly on cards. New illustrations use simple color; do not expose a separate style option. Automatic keeps suitable emoji and library pictures and generates only for gaps; library-only and emoji-only sources avoid it. Do not suggest AI illustrations on a completed draft: suitable emoji and library pictures are finished output. Deliberate picture changes remain available in Picture settings; only actual gaps need recovery guidance. Remove the legacy consistency switch. Sending or recording in the workspace starts directly with no opt-in interstitial. Keep photos and explicitly selected pictures stable until the parent includes them in a change.

Prepare pictures in a reviewable draft with cancellable progress. Keep the best available emoji when clearer artwork is unavailable and explain fallback in one concise summary. Contain illustrations within the tile so identifying features stay visible; personal photos retain their existing crop treatment. Credits and provider information belong in parent controls. Child playback never starts picture generation or changes saved pictures. See [picture contracts](PICTURES.md).
