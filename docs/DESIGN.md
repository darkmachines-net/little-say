# Little Say interface

Little Say is a touch-first soundboard app for children and the people supporting them. New devices start with language selection and optional starter-board generation. Existing libraries open to saved boards and their Play actions. Creation lives behind New board; editing lives with the board or tile it changes.

- Use the shared tokens in `apps/mobile/assets/brand/colors.json` through `src/ui/theme.ts`: deep evergreen text, a forest-green action color, a warm cream background, and six bright tile colors. White groups separate editing controls without heavy borders or shadows. The same palette colors the native launch screen and installed web app.
- Little Say's mountain is a friendly, rounded, wooded peak with a smaller foothill and a golden trail toward the top. Its smile makes the challenge welcoming. This is a playful reference to Little Si and Mount Si, not a literal trail map. Use the mascot with the Little Say wordmark in library headers, and on the launch/loading screen and empty library. It is decorative, never a navigation button or another tile. Keep the child's board free of additional branding chrome. Do not add a tagline, achievement system, or progress metaphor to the usable interface.
- Use the system sans-serif font. Titles are bold and compact. Rounded tiles, generous targets, and familiar pictures provide the playful character.
- Name the action or content directly: New board, Edit tile, Saved voices, Play languages. No slogans, marketing subtitles, decorative eyebrows, or landing-page sections.
- Use inclusive labels such as **Settings**, not role-specific labels such as **Parent settings**. Teachers, family members, friends and others can help create and manage boards.
- Keep the latest played board in a larger card above the regular grid. Its preview and Play again button both start play; keep Edit and the board menu available. A filled heart marks a favorite. Pin favorites immediately below the spotlight, with no duplicate spotlight card, then show other boards; use recent play order within each group. Use the board's actual tiles and title without promotional copy. Favorites and play history are device metadata, independent of content edits, copies, and undo.
- Show explanation where it helps a decision: first-use AI disclosure, missing translations, storage failures. Keep recurring controls concise.
- Call the feature **Account** or **Little Say account**, with actions such as **Create account**. Lead with saving boards and media to the account for access on other devices and recovery after a reinstall; explain in supporting text that Little Say also works without an account. Offer restore during setup and account controls in Settings. Explain photo/audio uploads before enabling sync, provide a recovery code, and show backup status separately from successful local saving. Preserve local boards after sign-out and keep account prompts out of child playback.
- Tapping outside a modal follows its close/cancel action; taps and gestures inside it do not dismiss it. Dismissing the leave-board confirmation keeps play active, and only an explicit confirmation leaves the board. AI dialogs retain their save/consent dismissal guards and release pending requests or microphone capture when closed.
- First-time setup has two functional steps: languages and starter boards. Save completed drafts for resuming, show the generated words, and offer a manual start when AI is unavailable. Never replace existing libraries with sample content or advance past a failed save.
- AI creation and editing open a conversation modal. Keep the transcript scrollable and the message/voice controls fixed above the keyboard. Show honest animated waiting feedback with stop/retry, then a reply and compact draft preview. Allow clarifications and refinements in place, with an explicit final save. Respect reduced motion.
- Keep the board editor on one tablet page: a compact responsive tile preview with AI, add, audio, and board actions beside it in landscape or below it in portrait. Tile position controls belong in the tile dialog. Small windows may scroll the preview without hiding the action controls.
- Use the platform photo picker and square cropper on iOS/Android. Browser uploads and later adjustments use direct drag/pinch interaction with fine zoom. Show the actual selected photo region in a large tile preview, and preserve that square region across thumbnails, play layouts, and device rotation. Crop cancellation must leave the previous image untouched; duplicate cards keep independent framing.
- Play prioritizes the tile grid. Its single-row header has the board title, a segmented language toggle when configured, and Hold to unlock. Keep the language toggle immediately left of the lock control. Playback state is shown on the tiles, not repeated in header text.
- Speech uses a forest-green outer outline and speaker badge. A touch briefly shows an amber inset outline. Both can be visible at once without changing geometry.
- Preserve large touch targets, visible selection states, native accessibility labels, forgiving swipe activation, and the separate hold/release/confirm exit sequence across platforms.
- The unlock hold's readiness label and release validation must use the same gesture state and monotonic clock. Cancel its timer and reset feedback together on movement, extra touches, native cancellation, backgrounding, or board interaction. Never announce release readiness from an independent timer.

Keep photos, user labels, and actual boards visually prominent. Add interface text only when it helps someone use the app.

## Pictures

Keep picture controls in editing flows: a compact conversation settings button, a board Pictures panel, and expandable card Picture options. Show Picture source as one labeled selector with a current-value explanation. Expand choices vertically with descriptions for Automatic, Library only, AI illustrations, and Emoji only. Do not use wrapping option chips. Keep boolean controls separate as switches, and show the inherited source explicitly on cards. New illustrations use simple color; do not expose a separate style option. Consistency is on by default. Keep photos and explicitly selected pictures stable until the user includes them in a change.

Prepare pictures in a reviewable draft with cancellable progress. Display unresolved cases plainly without substituting misleading generic symbols. Contain illustrations within the tile so identifying features stay visible; personal photos retain their existing crop treatment. Credits and provider information belong in editing controls. Child playback never starts picture generation or changes saved pictures. See [picture contracts](PICTURES.md).
